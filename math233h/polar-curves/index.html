From polar to Cartesian

The tangent comes from
the actual derivative.

x = r cos θ,   y = r sin θ

x′ = r′ cos θ − r sin θ

y′ = r′ sin θ + r cos θ

These formulas remain valid for negative r. The parametrization is regular exactly when r² + (r′)² > 0, provided r and r′ are finite. At a simple zero of r, the tangent is the radial line θ; different passages of a rose through the pole can have different tangents. If x′ ≠ 0, the slope is y′/x′. If x′ = 0 and y′ ≠ 0, the tangent is vertical. A zero velocity needs a separate limiting calculation.

A bridge to multivariable calculus

Polar coordinates reappear as a change of variables: dA = ρ dρ dθ, with radial distance ρ ≥ 0. Along a polar curve, swept area satisfies dA/dθ = r²/2; tracing a region twice counts its area twice.

Domains, numerical rendering & mathematical sources

Curves are sampled from the displayed analytic formulas; no formula text is executed. All parameters use constrained controls. Tangents use analytic derivatives. The pale curve is the selected interval; the green curve is the portion traced so far. Domain gaps are never connected by line segments.

The lemniscate uses the nonnegative square root on each allowed angular interval. At its pole, the polar derivative is unbounded; a dashed line shows the selected branch’s limiting tangent. At a cardioid cusp, the velocity is zero and a dashed line shows the limiting tangent. The exact point and derivative values are independent of the canvas sampling.

For Kepler conics, p > 0, e ≥ 0, and 1 + e cos(θ − φ) > 0. The axis angle φ rotates both the conic and the directrix x cos φ + y sin φ = p/e (when e > 0). Thus r is positive, and the hyperbola’s extra branch from squaring is excluded. The e = 0 circle has no finite directrix. Unbounded curves are clipped by a finite viewing window, not closed or cut off mathematically. “Fit curve” uses a bounded teaching window for unbounded examples.

The theory follows Jenia Tevelev’s Math 233H lecture on Kepler orbits and polar conics. Further references: OpenStax, Polar coordinates; OpenStax, Area and arc length; Wolfram MathWorld, Lemniscate. Angles are radians throughout. Curve lengths, geometric singularities, and clipping are not inferred from screen pixels.
