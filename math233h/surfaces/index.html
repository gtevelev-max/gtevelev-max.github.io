From an equation to a surface

A point lies on this surface when F(x,y,z) = 0. A coordinate trace substitutes one coordinate with a constant. At a regular point, ∇F is normal to the tangent plane; where ∇F = 0, a singularity may occur.

A finite view of an infinite object

The image is clipped to the cube −L ≤ x,y,z ≤ L. Flat-looking boundaries are cuts made by the view box. Increasing L reveals more of an unbounded surface; increasing mesh detail improves its approximation.

Singularities need mathematics

Meshes use marching tetrahedra and analytic gradient normals. Tiny holes, seams, rounded nodes, or missing isolated pieces can be numerical artifacts. Verified node markers come from exact coordinates, independently of the mesh, and remain visible through the surface. A singular point has no well-defined normal.

Classical equations
