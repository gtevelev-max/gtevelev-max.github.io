THE EXPERIMENT

Four points become eight.

Start with the centered hoop. Select Below, then At R = √2, then Above. Two maxima flatten, become local minima, and each gives rise to two new maxima.

On x² + y² = R², let p = xy.
f = R⁴ − 2p² − 4p + 1
The new branches have p = −1. They can appear only when R² ≥ 2.

The two same-sign diagonal points remain the global minima. At the threshold there are four distinct points; two are degenerate maxima.

What does λ measure?

At a stationary point, ∇f = λ∇g. With the center fixed and c = R², a differentiable stationary branch satisfies d f*/dc = λ. With radius as the resource instead, d f*/dR = 2Rλ.

A bead that is still moving has a normal coefficient λ̂, not a Lagrange multiplier. Its tangential residual shows the remaining first-order change.

Numerics and shifted hoops

Centered hoops use exact formulas, including the merging roots at √2. Moving the center uses a numerical polynomial root solver; residuals and candidate counts are displayed, but numerical output is not a proof of completeness near a merging root.

Constrained descent changes the angle with a line search, keeping the bead exactly on the hoop. It can settle at a local minimum. Compare all candidates to identify the global result.
