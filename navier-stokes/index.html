What the new simulation computes

This version numerically evaluates the inner-core equations in OpenAI’s September 2026 Navier–Stokes paper. It uses a finite radial Taylor expansion with smooth interpolation in height. The pressure prescribed on the axis is an explicit classroom choice.

This is a local approximation to one part of the construction. The complete solution needs the paper’s matched exterior, parameter hierarchy, and infinite correction scheme. The displayed flow is not a numerical realization of that complete solution, and its residual forcing is not certified smooth at t = 1.

1. Coordinates and velocity · (4.1)–(4.7)

A = ½ + h,   D = ½ − h
d = 1 − η²,   L = 1 − 2hη²
1 − t = qd,   z = qDη,   X = r²/(2q)
uθ = q−A√(2X) F
uz = q−AU,   r ur = Xv0
p = q−2AΠ
Ū = ∫01 U(sX, η) ds
v0 = (2ηU − 2DηŪ − dŪη)/L

The radial-velocity identity preserves ∇·u = 0 for the interpolated finite profiles. At η = 0, the radial scale is proportional to √(1 − t), while the speed prefactor is (1 − t)−A.

2. Inner equations · (4.13)

W = 1 − 2DηŪ − dŪη
H = Dη + dU
ℓ = 1 + XFX/F
Sq = −Wℓ − h(1 − 2ηU) − HFη/F
Sn = −WXUX − A(1 − 2ηU)U
− HUη − dΠη + 4AηΠ + 2ηXΠX
−2L(XFXX + 2FX) = FSq

These are the equations solved locally by the series. They contain the leading radial viscosity. The full Navier–Stokes momentum residual also involves axial viscosity and higher-order terms.

3. Axis data · (B.1)–(B.3)

U(0,η) = U* = 4η + j0
H* = Dη + dU*
ζ* = −LH*/(H*² + σ*²)
F(0,η) = exp(Λ∫0ηζ*(w) dw)/C

Here j0 = 0.025 and σ* = 0.5. C normalizes the largest axis value of F to 0.5. The h and Λ controls explore local profiles; they are not certified choices satisfying the paper’s full nested parameter restrictions.

Π(0,η) = −P/(1 + η²)²

This pressure datum is a demo choice. The paper’s Π0 comes from its globally matched outer profile; the formula above does not reproduce that matching.

4. Tracers, volume and time

This view starts with a rotating core already present. It demonstrates its prescribed similarity concentration, not vortex formation from rest. At h = 0.005, height divided by radius grows by only 4.7% before the display cutoff.

ω = ∇ × u
ωz(0,t) = 2(1 − t)−1−h F(0,0)

The vorticity readout measures axial rotation at the spatial origin. The value in parentheses is its ratio to t = 0. Increasing vorticity quantifies the strengthening local rotation as the core concentrates.

dX/dt = XW/(qL)
dη/dt = H/(qL)
dθ/dt = q−1−hF

Ruby tracers integrate these material equations with adaptive steps in ordinary time. A tracer that leaves the small displayed region is replaced at its incoming radial boundary. Changing appearance or camera does not change particle speed. Moving the time slider seeds a new sample at the selected time.

The ruby curves are instantaneous streamlines: their tangent is parallel to the current velocity field, with arrows in the flow direction. They differ from particle histories because the velocity changes with time. The swirl is weaker than radial inflow with these numerical parameters; the curves show that actual balance.

The gold region is a window in similarity coordinates, not a free liquid surface. The physical view uses one spatial scale on all axes; “Follow shrinking core” zooms uniformly. The animation stops at t = 0.9999, before the singular limit.

The vector diagrams show the central transverse slice with a separate arrow scale for each field. Pressure force is −∇p, not the external forcing f.
