A QUICK FIELD GUIDE

One triangle. Three geometries.

Enter integers p, q, r from 2 to 100. The chamber has angles π/p, π/q, π/r. A sum 1/p + 1/q + 1/r greater than 1 gives a sphere, equal to 1 a Euclidean plane, and less than 1 a hyperbolic plane.

T and T′

This app uses T for the full reflection group and T′ for its index-two, orientation-preserving subgroup. The prime means the even-word subgroup here, not the commutator subgroup. Conventions in books can differ.

Mirrors a, b, c are opposite the vertices with angles π/p, π/q, π/r. In T′, x = bc, y = ca, z = ab. Thus xᵖ = yᑫ = zʳ = xyz = 1. A T′ fundamental region is the union of a chamber and its a-neighbor. Its internal mirror disappears, and the whole two-chamber region has one fill and one outline. T′ has half as many fundamental domains as T.

One word, two readings

For a written word abc, the usual left action applies c, then b, then a in the three fixed original mirrors. The images of the starting chamber D are D, cD, bcD, abcD. The blue path joins their centers; it is a reflection trajectory and its chambers need not be adjacent.

The gold gallery reads a, then b, then c, crossing labeled walls of the current moving chamber. Its chambers are D, aD, abD, abcD. If the current chamber is gD, crossing its s-wall gives gsD: the physical reflection in that transported wall is gsg⁻¹. The two readings end at exactly the same chamber abcD.

Use Compare readings to show both, or isolate either one. Blue dashed lines are the original mirrors; the brighter blue line is the fixed mirror used at the current step. The gold line is the transported mirror used by the gallery. At step zero, the first mirrors are previewed. Gold dots and blue rings mark the two current chamber centers. On the sphere, a path can pass behind the globe; drag to inspect it.

T′ generators expand into two reflections before either reading: x = bc is read b, c in moving mirrors and c, b in fixed mirrors. Odd steps show an intermediate triangular chamber; completed pairs show a T′ domain. Use uppercase X, Y, Z or ^-1 for inverses. Parentheses and integer powers are supported. No cancellations are made, so relation words can be followed reflection by reflection.

What the colors mean

In the ordinary T view, the two triangle fills record even and odd reflection parity. In T′, cell, and lizard views, neighboring regions are assigned different colors to make their boundaries easy to see. Those tile colors do not encode a word, an element order, or a subgroup. Gold and blue always distinguish the two word readings.

Move around

Drag the coral point or the background. On the sphere this rotates the globe; in the disc it applies a hyperbolic isometry, keeping the boundary fixed. Arrow keys also move the view. The sphere shows its front hemisphere; rotate to see the rest. The disc displays a finite, adaptively regenerated patch. Very small boundary chambers are omitted.

Cells and their subdivisions

In T′, choose a cell subdivision to collect all triangles meeting a vertex into a larger cell. At (2,3,5), the q-vertex cells form an icosahedron with 20 triangular faces, and the r-vertex cells form its dual dodecahedron with 12 pentagonal faces. Their edges are projected onto the sphere. The p-vertex cells form 30 rhombi. Show reflection triangles reveals the smaller chambers inside each cell; hide them to see the polygonal cells clearly.

The same construction works in the plane and disc: (2,3,6) gives triangular or hexagonal cells; (2,3,7) gives triangular or heptagonal cells. Each vertex of order m has 2m chambers around it. These larger cells have rotational stabilizers, so they are not fundamental domains for T′. The bright outline still marks a T′ fundamental domain. Cell boundaries can also be shown over the lizards.

Chancay-inspired gauze

Escher mode uses interlocking reptile-shaped tiles with an open mesh, angular limbs, heavy contour threads, and natural cotton colors. The woven treatment is inspired by Chancay gauze textiles, including the Met’s cotton headcloth with interlocking figures. These lizards are an original interpretation, not a reproduction of a historical textile.

Shared boundaries still match exactly under the group’s rotations: protrusions and recesses interlock. The mesh moves with each tile and becomes simpler near the hyperbolic boundary. A single reflection chamber must keep its mirror boundaries, so the lizard pattern follows T′ in both group views. The bright outline follows the selected group: a triangle for T, a two-chamber lizard tile for T′.
